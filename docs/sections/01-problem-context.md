### 1.1. Food Security and Tropical Greenhouse Challenges

Singapore relies on imports for over 90% of its food, highlighting the importance of local agriculture for food security. Singapore Food Agency (SFA) strategy aims to increase local farming to supply 20% of domestic fibre consumption by 2035, up from 8% in 2025 (SFA, 2026). However, challenges arise from Singapore’s tropical climate, with temperatures rising about 0.25°C per decade, nearly double the global average (MSS, 2026).

While greenhouses can shield crops from rain and pests, they require effective thermal management due to high solar exposure and ambient temperatures. Additionally, greenhouse temperatures and humidity can vary spatially, depending on ventilation and cooling configurations (Cesar et al., 2020). Maintaining optimal growing conditions through mechanical cooling can lead to substantial operating costs, impacting commercial viability (He et al., 2024). Thus, greenhouse management must balance optimal growing conditions with cooling efficiency and costs.
