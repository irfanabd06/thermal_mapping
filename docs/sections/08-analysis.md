---
title: 8. Analysis of Results
---
**Spatial variation.** The 4.0 °C spread in the survey is about 12 times the SHT45's bench error (0.33 °C RMSE), so a grid of these sensors can resolve the spatial differences that matter, provided the sensors are shielded. The pattern of warm main plant zones and cooler germination and seedling zones supports the claim that one monitoring point is not representative. However, if the survey points were measured one after another, the spread also includes change over time, so the result is treated as indicative until it is repeated with simultaneous loggers.

**Measurement bias.** The unshielded bias in the greenhouse (+4.3 °C) is as large as the spatial spread itself. Without shielding, sensor heating would be indistinguishable from real hotspots. This makes R2 a critical requirement and the enclosure the first Semester 2 priority.

**Canopy sensing.** The MLX90640 tracks the centre temperature of the reference camera but underestimates peak surface temperatures. It will therefore be used for zone-average canopy temperature rather than detecting small hot spots.

**Implications for the approach.** Clouds and misting change conditions within minutes, as the sharp steps in Figure 16 show. This supports evaluating solutions against a baseline measured under comparable conditions, rather than against absolute temperature targets.
